Dhabiha vs. standard halal

Most halal questions are about ingredients like gelatin or alcohol. Meat raises a different question: not just which animal, but how it was slaughtered. That’s where dhabiha comes in.

Updated October 18, 2026

Short answerDhabiha (also spelled zabiha or zabihah) is the Islamic method of slaughter. Some Muslims only eat meat and poultry that was slaughtered this way; others also accept meat from Jews and Christians (People of the Book) or ordinary meat that isn’t pork. The ingredient rules — no pork, no alcohol — are the same either way.

What dhabiha slaughter involves

  • The animal is of a permitted kind (for example cattle, sheep, goats or chicken — never pigs).
  • The name of Allah is said at the time of slaughter (tasmiyah).
  • A swift cut with a sharp knife severs the throat, windpipe and jugular veins.
  • The blood is drained from the body.

Why Muslims differ on meat

The Qur’an permits “the food of those who were given the Scripture” (5:5), and scholars interpret this differently. Some accept meat slaughtered by Jews and Christians; others require Islamic slaughter with the name of Allah. There are also differences over machine slaughter and stunning. As a result, Muslims follow different practices — and both are common.

Standard vs. Dhabiha in practice

For packaged food, the difference shows up with animal ingredients:

  • Standard: avoids pork, alcohol and other clearly haram ingredients, and flags doubtful ones. Meat and poultry that aren’t pork are accepted unless shown otherwise.
  • Dhabiha: also avoids meat, poultry and animal-derived ingredients — broth, beef gelatin, tallow, collagen, whey from animal rennet — unless the product is halal-certified, because the slaughter can’t be confirmed from the label.

How Halal Pal handles both

Halal Pal asks which standard you follow when you first open it, and you can switch any time in Settings. In Dhabiha mode, chicken, beef and other animal ingredients are flagged unless the product carries a halal certification; in Standard mode, they aren’t, while pork, alcohol and doubtful ingredients are flagged in both.

Frequently asked questions

Is zabiha the same as dhabiha?

Yes. Dhabiha, zabiha and zabihah are different spellings of the same Arabic word for Islamic slaughter.

Is all halal meat dhabiha?

Certified halal meat is generally slaughtered according to Islamic requirements, but certifiers differ on details such as stunning and machine slaughter. Check the certifier’s standards if this matters to you.

Which standard should I choose?

Follow the practice of your community or scholar. Halal Pal supports both, and you can switch at any time.

This guide is general information, not a religious ruling. Scholars sometimes differ — when in doubt, consult a qualified scholar or a certified halal authority.
