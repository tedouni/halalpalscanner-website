Are mono- and diglycerides halal?

Mono- and diglycerides are among the most common additives in packaged food — in bread, ice cream, margarine, peanut butter and baked goods. Whether they’re halal depends on the fat they’re made from.

Updated October 18, 2026

Short answerMono- and diglycerides are halal when made from plant oils (like soybean or palm) and haram when made from pork fat. When the label doesn’t say — which is most of the time — they’re considered doubtful (mushbooh).

What are mono- and diglycerides?

They’re emulsifiers: they help oil and water mix, keep bread soft and stop ice cream from separating. They’re made by combining glycerol with fatty acids, and those fatty acids come from either plant oils (soybean, palm, canola, sunflower) or animal fats (beef tallow or pork lard). In Europe they’re labelled E471.

Reading the label

Label saysStatus
Vegetable mono- and diglycerides, mono- and diglycerides (soy / palm)Halal
Mono- and diglycerides (no source), E471Mushbooh
Mono- and diglycerides (pork / lard)Haram

Related additives

Several emulsifiers are made from mono- and diglycerides and share the same question: E472a–f (including DATEM, E472e, common in bread), polysorbates (E432–E436) and stearates. See the E-numbers guide for the full list.

Where you’ll find them

  • Sliced bread, buns and tortillas
  • Ice cream and whipped toppings
  • Margarine and spreads
  • Peanut butter, cakes, cookies and frosting
  • Chewing gum and some candies

Frequently asked questions

Is E471 halal or haram?

It depends on the source. Plant-based E471 is halal, pork-based E471 is haram, and E471 with no stated source is doubtful (mushbooh).

Are mono- and diglycerides vegan?

Only when made from plant oils. They can also be made from animal fat, and labels often don’t say which.

How can I tell if mono- and diglycerides are from pork?

The label usually won’t say. Look for “vegetable” mono- and diglycerides, halal certification, or ask the manufacturer.

This guide is general information, not a religious ruling. Scholars sometimes differ — when in doubt, consult a qualified scholar or a certified halal authority.
