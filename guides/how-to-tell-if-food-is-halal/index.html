How to tell if food is halal

Most packaged food doesn’t say whether it’s halal. Here’s a simple way to check any product — and the common mistakes, like assuming kosher or vegetarian means halal.

Updated October 18, 2026

Short answerFirst look for a halal certification symbol. If there isn’t one, check the ingredients for pork, alcohol, gelatin and insect-derived additives, then for doubtful ingredients like natural flavors or E471 whose source isn’t stated. A halal scanner app does all of this in seconds.

1. Look for halal certification

A halal certification symbol means an independent body has checked the ingredients, their sources and the production process. It’s the most reliable sign. Certifiers differ in their standards — for example on stunning or machine slaughter — so if that matters to you, check which body certified the product.

2. Check for clearly haram ingredients

  • Pork in any form: pork, lard, bacon, ham, pepperoni, salami, prosciutto. Is pepperoni halal?
  • Alcohol: wine, beer, rum, brandy, liqueur, added ethanol.
  • Gelatin without a halal source. Is gelatin halal?
  • Blood and blood products.
  • Insect-derived additives: carmine (E120) and shellac (E904) — scholars differ.

3. Watch for doubtful ingredients

Some ingredients can be plant- or animal-derived, and labels rarely say which. These are mushbooh (doubtful):

  • Natural flavors and vanilla extract
  • Mono- and diglycerides (E471), polysorbates, DATEM
  • Enzymes and rennet in cheese and whey
  • L-cysteine (E920) in bread, glycerin, stearates

Common mistakes

AssumptionWhy it’s not enough
“It’s kosher, so it’s halal”Kosher allows alcohol (like wine) and its slaughter rules differ from halal.
“It’s vegetarian, so it’s halal”Vegetarian food can contain alcohol, vanilla extract or carmine.
“No pork listed, so it’s fine”Pork can hide in gelatin, E471, L-cysteine or enzymes.
“It’s beef, so it’s halal”Beef is only halal for many Muslims if the animal was slaughtered Islamically. Dhabiha vs. standard

Frequently asked questions

Is kosher food halal?

Not automatically. Kosher rules allow some things halal doesn’t — notably alcohol such as wine — and kosher and halal slaughter requirements differ. Check the ingredients or look for halal certification.

Is vegetarian food always halal?

No. Vegetarian products can still contain alcohol, vanilla extract or insect-derived additives like carmine.

What is the easiest way to check if food is halal?

Look for a halal certification symbol. If there isn’t one, scan the barcode or ingredient list with a halal scanner app, which checks every ingredient for you.

This guide is general information, not a religious ruling. Scholars sometimes differ — when in doubt, consult a qualified scholar or a certified halal authority.
