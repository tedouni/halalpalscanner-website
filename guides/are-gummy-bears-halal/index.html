Are gummy bears and marshmallows halal?

Gummies, marshmallows and jelly desserts are some of the most common foods Muslims check — and with good reason. Most are made with gelatin.

Updated October 18, 2026

Short answerMost regular gummy bears, marshmallows and gelatin desserts are not halal, because they contain gelatin that’s often from pork. Versions made with beef or fish gelatin from a halal source, or with pectin, agar or starch instead, can be halal.

Why sweets are tricky

Gelatin gives gummies their chew and marshmallows their fluff. When the label just says “gelatin”, it’s very often pork. More on gelatin sources

Halal alternatives

  • Halal-certified gummies, usually made with beef gelatin from halal-slaughtered cattle.
  • Vegan or vegetarian gummies made with pectin or agar — still check for carmine and natural flavors.
  • Vegan marshmallows made without gelatin.

Frequently asked questions

Are Haribo gummy bears halal?

Standard Haribo gummies sold in many countries contain pork gelatin, but Haribo also makes halal-certified versions for some markets. Check the label or scan the barcode.

Is jello halal?

Most gelatin desserts use gelatin that’s often pork-derived, so they’re not halal unless made with halal gelatin or a plant-based alternative like agar.

Are marshmallows halal?

Most marshmallows contain gelatin, often from pork. Halal-certified marshmallows (with beef gelatin) and vegan marshmallows are available.

This guide is general information, not a religious ruling. Scholars sometimes differ — when in doubt, consult a qualified scholar or a certified halal authority.
