How to scan food to check if it’s halal

Reading every ingredient list yourself is slow, and the tricky ingredients — gelatin, natural flavors, E471, carmine — are easy to miss. A halal food scanner does the checking for you in seconds.

Updated October 18, 2026

Short answerOpen a halal scanner app like Halal Pal Scanner, point your phone at the product’s barcode, and you’ll get a Halal, Mushbooh or Haram verdict with the reasons. No barcode, or the product isn’t found? Scan the ingredient list instead.

Step 1: Scan the barcode

Open Halal Pal Scanner and tap Start scanning. Point the camera at the barcode on the package — it reads it automatically and looks the product up in databases covering millions of products, including Open Food Facts and the USDA.

You’ll see a verdict in a second or two:

VerdictWhat it means
HalalNo prohibited ingredients found, or the product carries a halal-certified label.
MushboohDoubtful — an ingredient’s source isn’t stated. What mushbooh means
HaramContains a forbidden ingredient such as pork, alcohol or unverified gelatin.

The exact ingredients that caused the verdict are highlighted, and you can tap each one to see why it was flagged.

Step 2: No barcode? Scan the ingredient list

For products that aren’t in any database — imported foods, bakery items, local brands — switch to Ingredients mode. Take a photo of the ingredient list, drag the corners around it, and the halal scanner reads every ingredient on your phone. Photos are never uploaded.

Step 3: Choose your standard

Muslims differ on meat and poultry. In Standard mode the scanner flags pork, alcohol and doubtful ingredients; Dhabiha mode also flags meat and animal ingredients without halal certification. Dhabiha vs. standard halal

Tips for scanning halal food

  • Good light helps — the flash turns on automatically if a barcode can’t be read.
  • Zoom in or out with a pinch, or tap 1× / 2×, to frame small barcodes or long ingredient lists.
  • Shopping for a whole cart? Continuous scan (Premium) checks one product after another without stopping, with a different vibration for each verdict.
  • Everything is saved in your scan history, with product photos, search and filters.

What a halal scanner checks for

  • Pork and its derivatives — pork, lard, bacon, ham, pepperoni and more
  • Alcohol — wine, beer, spirits and added ethanol
  • Gelatin — and whether a halal source is named. Is gelatin halal?
  • Insect-derived additives — carmine (E120) and shellac (E904)
  • Doubtful ingredients — natural flavors, E471, enzymes, L-cysteine and others. See the E-numbers list

Frequently asked questions

Is there an app that scans food for halal?

Yes. Halal Pal Scanner is a free halal scanner app for iPhone. It scans barcodes and ingredient lists and tells you whether food is Halal, Mushbooh or Haram, with the reasons.

Can I scan food without a barcode?

Yes. Take a photo of the ingredient list in Ingredients mode and the app checks every ingredient — useful for imported or bakery products that aren’t in any database.

Is a halal scanner always right?

A scanner checks product data and ingredient lists, which can be incomplete or change. It’s a fast guide; when in doubt, look for halal certification or ask a qualified authority.

This guide is general information, not a religious ruling. Scholars sometimes differ — when in doubt, consult a qualified scholar or a certified halal authority.
