Is L-cysteine halal?

L-cysteine helps commercial bread, bagels and pizza dough stay soft and stretchy. Where it comes from is the surprising part.

Updated October 18, 2026

Short answerL-cysteine (E920) is doubtful. It’s often made from poultry feathers, sometimes from pig bristles or human hair, and increasingly by fermentation. Fermentation-made and synthetic L-cysteine is halal; human hair–derived L-cysteine is considered impermissible.

What L-cysteine is

L-cysteine is an amino acid used as a dough conditioner — it makes dough easier to work with in commercial baking. You’ll find it in some bread, bagels, pizza crusts, tortillas and fast-food buns. On labels it appears as L-cysteine, cysteine or E920.

How to avoid it

  • Choose halal-certified bread and baked goods.
  • Look for bakeries that state their L-cysteine is plant- or fermentation-based.
  • Check the ingredient list for E920 or L-cysteine.

Frequently asked questions

Is E920 halal?

E920 (L-cysteine) is doubtful unless its source is stated. Fermentation-made L-cysteine is halal; animal or human hair sources aren’t.

Is L-cysteine in bread from human hair?

Some L-cysteine has historically been made from human hair, though feathers and fermentation are now more common. Labels rarely say which.

Is L-cysteine vegan?

Only when made by fermentation or synthetically. Feather- and hair-derived L-cysteine isn’t vegan.

This guide is general information, not a religious ruling. Scholars sometimes differ — when in doubt, consult a qualified scholar or a certified halal authority.
